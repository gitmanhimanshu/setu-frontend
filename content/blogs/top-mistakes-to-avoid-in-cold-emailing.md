## Why Is Everyone Ignoring You?

You’ve curated the perfect list of contacts. You’ve sent out 100 emails. And... crickets. 

If your response rate is hovering near zero, the problem usually isn't the product you're pitching or the skills you're offering. The problem is the email itself. 

Here are the top three mistakes people make in cold outreach, and how to fix them.

### Mistake 1: The "Me, Me, Me" Email

The most common mistake in cold emailing is focusing entirely on yourself. 

*“I am a developer. I have 5 years of experience. I want a job. I am looking for opportunities.”*

The hard truth is that the recipient doesn't care about you yet. They care about their own problems. 

**The Fix:** Flip the script. Focus on them. 
Instead of: *“I have 5 years of React experience.”*
Try: *“I noticed your engineering team is scaling quickly. With my background in React, I can help your team ship features faster without adding management overhead.”*

### Mistake 2: The Essay

When people are nervous about cold emailing, they tend to over-explain. They write a five-paragraph essay detailing their entire work history, hoping that *something* in that wall of text will stick.

Nobody reads long cold emails. They get archived immediately.

**The Fix:** Be ruthless with your editing. A cold email should rarely exceed 100-150 words. You are not trying to close the deal or get the job in the first email. You are simply trying to start a conversation. 

### Mistake 3: The Weak Call to Action (CTA)

How do you end your emails? 
*“Let me know what you think.”* 
*“Hope to hear from you.”*

These are terrible CTAs because they put the mental burden on the recipient. What are they supposed to think about? What do you want them to do?

**The Fix:** Be specific, low-friction, and clear. 
*“Are you open to a 5-minute chat this Thursday?”*
*“Is this something your team is currently focusing on?”*

Make it easy for them to reply with a simple "Yes."
